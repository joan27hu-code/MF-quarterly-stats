                                                              Demand Continues to Outpace Supply

                                                              Multifamily demand strengthened in Q2 2026, with net absorption totaling 152,856 units, more
                                                              than double the 67,268 units delivered during the quarter. That momentum helped push
                                                              occupancy higher and reinforced the resilience of renter demand despite slower economic
                                                              growth and elevated borrowing costs. Structural affordability constraints in the for-sale housing
                                                              market remain supportive of leasing activity, as high home prices, elevated mortgage rates, and
                                                              limited ownership options keep many households in the rental pool longer.

                                                              While demand remains below the extraordinary levels reached during the post-pandemic
                                                              surge, the second quarter marked an important step toward a healthier supply-demand
                                                              balance. Absorption was strong enough to offset new deliveries nationwide, suggesting that
                                                              recent supply pressures are becoming more manageable. That progress is especially notable
                                                              given the still-elevated development pipeline, though performance remains uneven across
                                                              markets.

                                                              Markets with significant recent construction continue to face longer lease-up timelines and
                                                              more competitive conditions, while supply-constrained coastal and Midwest markets are
                                                              benefiting from steadier demand and limited new inventory. As development activity slows and
                                                              fewer projects move through the pipeline, sustained absorption should help further stabilize
                                                              occupancy and support a gradual recovery in rent growth through the remainder of 2026.

                                                      Q2 2026 Spotlight:
                                                      U.S. Affordable Housing

                                                      The U.S. affordable housing market stands at a pivotal moment in 2026.
                                                      Rising home prices, elevated borrowing costs, and rent growth that has
                                                      outpaced wages have widened the gap between household income and
                                                      housing costs, keeping more people renting for longer and fueling demand
                                                      for income-restricted and workforce housing.

                                                      That pressure has reached record levels. According to the Harvard Joint
                                                      Center for Housing Studies' America's Rental Housing 2024, about half of all
                                                      U.S. renter households are now cost-burdened. Roughly 26%, or some 12
                                                      million households, are severely cost-burdened, spending more than half
                                                      their income on housing. These strains fall hardest on lower-income renters,
                                                      underscoring the depth of unmet need.

                                                      Supply, however, is struggling to keep pace. New affordable deliveries
                                                      recently reached a cyclical high, driven by expanded public funding and years
                                                      of federal, state, and local efforts to address the nation's housing shortage.
                                                      Even so, the same headwinds facing the broader multifamily sector, including
                                                      elevated construction costs and higher interest rates, are making new
                                                      development increasingly difficult.

                                                      Still, affordable housing remains one of the most resilient multifamily
                                                      subsectors. Durable demand fundamentals, along with growing focus among
                                                      developers, investors, and policymakers on preserving and expanding the
                                                      nation's affordable inventory, continue to support the segment.

                                                                                        Affordability Gap Fuels Demand

                                                                                        Barriers to homeownership and the growing number of cost-burdened renters have
                                                                                        expanded the pool of households eligible for affordable housing. Although it declined
                                                                                        over the past year, the median U.S. single-family home sale price ballooned by 34.5%
                                                                                        over the past 10 years to $403,200, outpacing median household income growth of
                                                                                        13.9%. As a result, the median home price-to-income ratio increased to 4.7, meaning a
                                                                                        typical home costs nearly five times the median annual income.

                                                                                        Home price-to-income ratios are significantly elevated in high-cost markets like San Jose
                                                                                        (11.3), Los Angeles (10.5), San Francisco (9.8), and Miami (7.6). As a result, many would-
                                                                                        be first-time buyers are delaying homeownership and opting to rent. At the same time,
                                                                                        nearly half of all renters spend more than 30% of their income on housing, while 26%
                                                                                        are severely cost-burdened. In addition to these affordability challenges, a wave of
                                                                                        Americans entering retirement on fixed incomes and persistent job growth in low-wage
                                                                                        service sectors are expected to sustain long-term demand for affordable housing.

  Policy Response
                                                                                                      Looking Ahead
 Policy measures are being enacted at the federal, state, and local levels to preserve existing units, fund new development, a nd address the affordable housing
 shortage. The Low-Income Housing Tax Credit is being permanently expanded this year through increased credit allocations and a lower private activity bond
 financing threshold. These changes could lead to the development of more than 1 million new affordable units over the next 10 years.

 The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, which became law in July 2026, will also help remove some barriers to new development. Key provisions include funding
 for local governments that add new housing through zoning reform or streamlined permitting, higher FHA multifamily loan limits, and direct use of Community
 Development Block Grant funds for new affordable housing construction.

 Reforms across many states and municipalities are already underway to address the supply shortage. Many states are adopting or expanding their own versions
 of the federal Low-Income Housing Tax Credit program, including Michigan, Maine, and California. Others are targeting developmen t barriers more directly. Texas
 enacted a statewide reform last year allowing apartments to be built in commercial zones, while a Georgia law passed in May r equires counties and municipalities
 to follow strict review timelines to speed up development. Earlier this year, New York City launched a $4 billion initiative to produce affordable housing and
 preserve existing units. While these measures represent meaningful progress, significant hurdles to new development remain. The coming years will test how
 effectively these policies can narrow the gap between supply and demand.
