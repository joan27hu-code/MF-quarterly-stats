High Rates Limiting Transactions; New Mortgages 50% Greater Than In-Place
The 30-year fixed mortgage rate climbed to 6.49% in 2Q26, while the effective interest rate on outstanding mortgage debt rose to 4.33%, a spread of 216 basis points, or 50% higher
on a relative basis, reflecting the mortgage rate lock-in effect. Assuming the 30-year rate holds steady, the effective rate's roughly 0.24% annual pace of increase since its 2022 low
implies it would take about 8 to 9 years to converge with current rates. This prolonged gap suggests sustained pressure on homebuying that could, in turn, benefit multifamily.

Starts Continue to Tail Off as Backlog Between Approval and Groundbreaking Grows
The widening spread between multifamily permits and starts, now the largest since 2010, reflects a feasibility standoff rather than a construction slowdown. Elevated costs and tighter
financing have made many permitted projects uneconomical to start, while lingering oversupply from the 2022–2024 delivery boom removes any urgency to break ground into soft
rents.

Negative Leverage Deals Have Continued to Recede
As base rates have increased, interest rates for CRE lending have also increased, although cap rates have been slower to react. Multifamily and industrial, where fundamentals have
been the strongest, continue to have the tightest cap rate-to-interest rate spreads, but all property types are being squeezed. Negative leverage has also become a lot more common in
recent years as investors rely on fundamentals to grow their NOI. However, as the market becomes more convinced that rates will remain higher-for-longer, spreads will become
dependent on the availability of debt capital.
